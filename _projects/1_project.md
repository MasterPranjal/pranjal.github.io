**Open Bike Commons** is an eight-week, community-led pilot that tests how practical bicycle and e-bike maintenance learning can strengthen access to cycling, develop green skills, preserve repair knowledge, and generate evidence to inform future practice and policy in Wales.

The project is developed in partnership with **Transport for Wales** and aligned with the Welsh Government's active travel and circular economy ambitions.

---

## The challenge

Access to a bicycle does not by itself ensure sustained use. People also need confidence, maintenance knowledge, appropriate tools, and trusted local support. Minor faults leave bicycles unused; unfamiliar tools, unclear instructions, and fear of causing damage discourage novice repairers — particularly young people and communities facing financial, geographic, or accessibility barriers.

<blockquote>
How might a community-led, inclusive and creative repair model equip people — especially young people — with the skills, confidence, shared knowledge and usable tools needed to maintain bicycles and e-bikes and participate in active travel?
</blockquote>

This challenge is broader than fixing individual bikes. It concerns building local capability to maintain, reuse and keep cycles in service — a repair-economy problem as much as a transport one.

---

## What the pilot does

Over eight weeks, a small, diverse cohort works on real bicycles in supervised sessions covering:

<div class="row mt-3">
  <div class="col-sm-6">
    <ul>
      <li>Basic safety checks</li>
      <li>Puncture repair</li>
      <li>Brake and gear adjustment</li>
      <li>Routine e-bike maintenance</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
  <div class="col-sm-6">
    <ul>
      <li>Tool identification and use</li>
      <li>Peer knowledge exchange</li>
      <li>Zine-making and visual repair guides</li>
      <li>Co-design of clearer tools and instructions</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</div>

Participants work on bicycles themselves rather than watching a mechanic. Creative documentation — drawings, photographs, process maps — serves both as a learning resource and as research evidence about how people understand repair.

---

## Research questions

1. How can community-based repair activity increase confidence and practical capability in bicycle and e-bike maintenance?
2. How is repair knowledge demonstrated, interpreted and transferred between experienced repairers and novice participants?
3. Can creative approaches, including zine-making, support reflection, documentation and wider sharing of repair knowledge?
4. How do the availability, layout, labelling and physical design of repair tools support or hinder novice participation?
5. What opportunities exist to redesign tools, toolkits or instructions to make repair safer, clearer and more inclusive?
6. What relationship, if any, is visible between repair confidence and readiness to cycle more frequently?
7. What minimum combination of venue, people, tools, bicycles and partnerships is required to repeat the model?

---

## Partnership with Transport for Wales

The pilot's baseline and follow-up surveys draw on the **Transport for Wales active travel survey instrument**, asking participants what would encourage them to cycle or walk as a means of transport, whether they own a cycle, and what barriers they currently face. This allows pilot findings to be situated within wider Welsh active travel data.

TfW staff are involved as observers and advisors. The pilot is also timed alongside the **Swansea Active Travel Network Map consultation** (open until 19 October 2026), and participants and community partners are encouraged to engage with that process directly.

## What it will produce

- A tested community repair and learning model
- A small evidence dataset on reach, skills, confidence, tool use and bicycle outcomes
- A participant-created zine or open knowledge resource
- A tool-usability findings report and initial human-centred design requirements
- An indicative delivery cost and resource model
- A recommendation on whether the approach should be stopped, adapted, repeated or extended
